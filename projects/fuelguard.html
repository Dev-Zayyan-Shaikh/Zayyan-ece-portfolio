Core Problem: Ethanol Phase Separation

Modern commercial petrol contains ethanol blends (e.g. E10 / E20). Ethanol is hygroscopic, meaning it absorbs moisture from ambient air and condensation inside fuel tanks.

When water saturation reaches a critical threshold, the ethanol separates from the hydrocarbon petrol matrix. Because the ethanol-water mixture is denser, it sinks to the bottom of the fuel tank. When drawn into the engine intake, this corrosive, low-octane water-rich layer causes severe misfires, stalling, and engine component failure.

Multi-Modal Sensing Approach

Commercial OEM flex-fuel sensors often cost ~₹8,000 and rely on complex high-frequency optical dielectric measurement. FuelGuard presents a robust, cost-effective alternative utilizing multi-modal sensing:

01 // CUSTOM ELECTRODE RESISTIVITY SENSOR

Pure petrol is a non-conductive dielectric with extremely high electrical resistance. When ethanol-water phase separation occurs at the bottom electrode probes, electrical resistivity drops sharply, causing a measurable voltage shift on the ESP8266 analog pin.

02 // OPTICAL LDR LIGHT TRANSMISSION

Complements electrical resistivity by projecting a focused light beam across the bottom liquid layer onto a Light Dependent Resistor (LDR). The refractive index shift between pure petrol and water-ethanol phase separation alters light transmission, providing dual-point validation.

Automated Extraction & Octane Restoring Mechanism

Upon confirming phase separation via dual sensing:

  1. Air-Pump Extraction: The ESP8266 activates a compact air pump. Utilizing pressure-assisted fluid displacement (Bernoulli's principle), the pump draws out only the dense lower ethanol-water layer through a dip tube.
  2. Dosing Pump Activation: Once sensor readings return to normal petrol thresholds, the air pump turns off and a secondary dosing pump injects a calculated measure of octane booster to restore the fuel blend.
